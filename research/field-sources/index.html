Which source carries each study, sample, experiment and run field¶

Question: for each field a study needs, which source carries it? Where two sources carry the same field, which one should win? And how much does the join from a GEO sample (GSM) to an SRA experiment (SRX) lose?

All checks ran on 2026-10-14 against live NCBI services and the bulk files dated 2026-10-14. Only metadata was read. Each table row has a mark:

  • measured: a live probe or a bulk query printed it on 2026-10-14.
  • read: an official page, a schema or an earlier note says it.
  • to-verify: needs a check not yet run. No row has this mark now.

Answer¶

  • The bulk GEO dump finds studies; SOFT describes them. The esummary dump of all 298,404 Series matches live family SOFT on title, summary, organism, platform, type, sample count, PubMed IDs, public date and the GSM list for 90 of 90 Series. It never carries SuperSeries links and has no sample fields beyond titles, so family SOFT is still needed.
  • GEO wins on text, SRA wins on reads. For a study with a GEO Series, the family SOFT file is the source for every text field. That covers titles, summary, design, characteristics, protocols, PubMed IDs, dates and SuperSeries links. SRA is the source for library layout, runs, spot and base counts and submitted files. GEO lacks all of these.
  • The library fields agree. GEO and SRA agree on library strategy, source, selection and instrument for 60 to 63 of 63 sampled samples. Every disagreement is a pre-2011 or reused third-party deposit, where the SRA record describes the reads. So SRA wins.
  • For an SRA-only study, BioProject and BioSample carry the text. The SRA STUDY title and abstract copy the BioProject. BioSample holds the sample attributes, with NCBI's harmonized_name beside each one.
  • BioSample is an annotation layer, not a copy of GEO. Only 233 of 325 sampled GEO characteristics (72%) reach BioSample with the same key and value. BioSample can be a stale snapshot, can belong to another submitter, and rewrites values (None becomes missing, Male becomes male). Keep the GEO text, and take only harmonized_name from BioSample.
  • The bulk files match the live services. Bulk bioproject.xml and biosample_set.xml.gz records equal the efetch replies field for field. The Parquet and SRA_Accessions.tab agree with the SRA XML on every compared field.
  • The Parquet is not complete. It lacks 971,528 of the 43,022,633 live public runs in SRA_Accessions.tab (2.3%), and it holds no suppressed or withdrawn runs. Use SRA_Accessions.tab for the list of runs and for exact spot and base counts.
  • The join. Of 72 random sequencing GSMs, 9 (12.5%) have no SRX. The other 63 each map to exactly one SRX. The SOFT Sample_relation = SRA: line finds all 63. In bulk, SRA_Accessions.tab aliases find 60. A GSM can map to several SRX: the sample had none, but GSE20301 and GSE19456 have them, and so do 450 of 5.6 million GSMs in the bulk aliases.

Field table¶

Source names used below:

  • SOFT: the GEO family SOFT file, the format chosen in the GEO format note.
  • SRA XML: efetch db=sra, one EXPERIMENT_PACKAGE per experiment.
  • Parquet: NCBI's SRA metadata table (sra.metadata), one row per run.
  • Accessions: SRA_Accessions.tab, one row per SRA object.
  • BioProject and BioSample: the bulk bioproject.xml and biosample_set.xml.gz, which equal the efetch replies.
  • esummary: esummary db=gds or db=sra.

The seqforge column says what seqforge's fetch_records reads today and what it wants next, from its list on issue #23. A dash means seqforge has not asked for the field.

Two probed studies¶

PRJNA1027859, SRA-only. Six experiments, one run each, 10x single-nucleus RNA-seq (TRANSCRIPTOMIC SINGLE CELL, PAIRED, NovaSeq 6000). The SRA STUDY title and abstract match the BioProject. The SRA sample has no TITLE. Its alias equals BioSample's "Sample name", and BioSample's own title is the package's generic text. SRA's SAMPLE_ATTRIBUTE list adds a BioSampleModel tag that BioSample does not list as an attribute. Every BioSample attribute has a harmonized_name, because the submitter used the model-organism package. In the Parquet, sex becomes the computed key sex_calc and geo_loc_name: not applicable is dropped. The bulk BioProject and BioSample records equal the efetch replies.

GSE60450, GEO. Twelve GSMs, twelve SRX, one run each, single-end HiSeq 2000. The series title, PubMed ID (25730472) and BioProject (PRJNA258286) match across SOFT, esummary, SRA and BioProject. The SRA center_name is GEO on every object. BioSample keeps the GEO characteristic keys as attribute_name and adds harmonized_name for 4 of the 6 keys. strain/background and immunophenotype get none. GEO's transcriptomic is SRA's TRANSCRIPTOMIC; the values differ only in case. In the Parquet each run also carries the GSM as the attribute geo_accession_exp.

A dropped package. One efetch db=sra call for the 12 GSE60450 experiments returned 10 packages without an error. Three repeats of the same call returned all 12. A reader of the SRA XML must check that every requested accession came back. seqforge's fetch_records does not check this today.

Does !Sample_relation carry the SRA and BioSample links¶

Method. The script docs/research/field-sources/soft_relations.py downloaded the family SOFT of all 90 Series touched by the GSM sample and read every sample in them: 60,724 GSMs submitted from 2008 to 2025.

Format. Each link is one line holding one accession in a fixed URL:

!Sample_relation = SRA: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sra?term=SRX681985
!Sample_relation = BioSample: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/biosample/SAMN02991530

Every SRA line held exactly one experiment accession: 41,995 SRX and 25 ERX. Every BioSample line held one SAMN. No other URL form appeared. A GSM with several experiments gets several SRA: lines. Other relation kinds seen were Reanalyzed by: GSE… and, for Roadmap Epigenomics samples, Named Annotation:.

Reliability.

  • BioSample: nearly always. 60,061 of the 60,339 samples of type SRA have a BioSample line (99.5%).
  • SRA: complete in most Series, absent in some. In 74 of the 90 Series every sample of type SRA has an SRA line. Per sample the rate is lower, 42,020 of 60,339 (70%), because a few large Series dominate the count. The 16 Series with gaps fall into four groups:
  • Plate-based single-cell Series with one GSM per cell. GSE158435 links 3,248 of 16,491 GSMs, and GSE94820 links none of 2,384.
  • Raw data held back: GSE285029 and GSE163634 have no SRA lines; GSE285029 has no BioSample lines either.
  • Roadmap Epigenomics (GSE16256, GSE17312, GSE19465) and 2009 to 2010 Series, partly linked.
  • SOFT missing a link SRA has. GSE175518 has no SRA line on any of its 942 GSMs, yet SRA holds a run under GSM5339608 in its SRP.
  • Family file and GSM record agree. For all 72 sampled GSMs the family file lists the same SRX as the per-GSM record.
  • Multi-SRX GSMs exist. GSE20301 (2010) has 6 GSMs with 3 or 4 SRX each, one per SRA: line, and 2 GSMs sequenced on 454 (GSM517436, GSM517440) of type SRA with no SRA line. GSE19456 has a GSM (GSM484820) with 2 SRX. Both were checked live with the same script.

How lossy the GSM to SRX join is¶

Method. The script docs/research/field-sources/gsm_srx_join.py produced the live numbers, run with --per-year 4 --seed 23. For each publication year from 2008 to 2025 it draws 4 random GSMs from gsm[ETYP] AND "high throughput sequencing"[PTYP] AND <year>[PDAT]. That gives 72 GSMs in 90 Series. For each GSM it reads the GSM SOFT record, follows elink gds→sra, fetches every linked SRX with efetch db=sra, and fetches the GSM's BioSample. The bulk numbers come from docs/research/field-sources/accessions_check.sh and accessions_compare.py, which look for the GSM in the Alias column of SRA_Accessions.tab.

Why 9 GSMs have no SRX.

  • Raw data never reached SRA: two 2009 GSMs with no supplementary files, and three human GSMs from 2017, 2021 and 2025. One of them, GSE285029, says "Due to our institution's security policy, we are unable to provide raw data."
  • Two 2011 Roadmap Epigenomics GSMs (GSE17312, GSE19465). They link to a project page outside NCBI, not to SRA.
  • One 2021 single-cell GSM with no SRA line, in a Series that has an SRP.
  • One 2022 GSM (GSM5339608) whose SRX exists, with the GSM as its alias, but SOFT omits the link. Only elink, an SRA search and the bulk aliases find it. Its only run has 8 spots.

Why the alias joins miss some. The 2008 to 2010 GEO deposits name the experiment freely, such as xrn1 rrp6 lsm1 pat1 Rep 4. Their runs still carry GSMn_1, so the run alias recovers six of them. Three stay lost to every alias route: a 2009 Roadmap GSM whose run alias is a lab name (renlab.2009-06-23.1), a 2010 GSM that points at an ArrayExpress deposit (ERX000501), and a 2014 GSM that points at an SRX another center submitted directly to SRA. The old deposits also carry a second, older BioSample on the SRA side. elink misses the same old and reused deposits. labdata's experiments_for follows elink, so it should miss them too (inferred, not run).

Across all of SRA. The aliases in SRA_Accessions.tab name 5,620,204 distinct GSMs. Of these, 5,619,754 map to exactly one SRX and 450 map to 2 to 9. The Parquet sample_name names 5,461,662 distinct GSMs, fewer, because the Parquet lacks some runs. A join built on the Parquet alone will report some Series as only partly joined when they join fully live.

Why characteristics do not round-trip. In the GSMs checked by hand:

  • The BioSample is older than the GEO record. GSM1599675 has 5 characteristics in SOFT but only 1 in BioSample.
  • BioSample rewrites values. None becomes missing (GSM6123703) and Male becomes male (GSM2632690).
  • The BioSample belongs to another submitter. GSM6783145 uses an ENCODE BioSample with different attribute names.
  • Some early GEO characteristics have no key. GSM340803 and GSM307158 (2008) carry a bare value with no key: part.

Caveats. The sample takes 4 GSMs from each year. Recent years hold most GSMs, so the pooled rates over-weight old deposits. For 2011 onward the rates are 7 of 60 with no SRX and 1 of 53 missed by the experiment alias. With n = 72 the 95% interval on 12.5% is about 7% to 22% (Wilson, inferred).

Parquet coverage. The script docs/research/field-sources/parquet_coverage.py joined the Parquet's 43,762,704 runs with the 43,022,633 live public runs in SRA_Accessions.tab. 971,528 live public runs are missing from the Parquet, spread over every year since 2007 and mostly from 2021 on. The bulk GEO Series dump¶

The bulk GEO download is esummary db=gds for every Series (gse[ETYP]), version 2.0 JSON, one record per line. Its manifest lists 298,404 query IDs and 298,404 records. The script docs/research/field-sources/geo_esummary_check.py streamed it and compared the 90 Series of the GSM sample with live family SOFT and live esummary.

Coverage. The dump holds 298,404 distinct Series. Live Entrez counted 298,404 the same day, and all 90 sampled Series are present. Live esummary returned the same value for every field of all 90 records, so nothing changed between the download and the check.

Across the whole dump: every record has a summary; 232,148 have PubMed IDs; 298,229 have a BioProject; 80,415 have an SRA link in extrelations; and the sample list length equals n_samples in 298,141 records. Three summaries end in ...; they were not checked against SOFT.

What the dump serves and what still needs SOFT.

  • The dump is enough to find studies. It can list every Series with its title, summary, organisms, platforms, assay type, sample count, PubMed IDs, BioProject, public date and GSM accessions and titles.
  • SOFT is still needed for the overall design, contributors, SuperSeries and SubSeries links, the submission and last-update dates, and every sample field beyond the title. That covers characteristics, source, protocols, library fields, data processing and the SRA and BioSample relations.
  • The dump cannot detect change. It has no last-update date; the [UDAT] search remains the change feed (discussion #17).
