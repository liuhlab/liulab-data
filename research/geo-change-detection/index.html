How a refresh finds changed GEO Series¶

Measured 2026-11-06 with small, gentle queries (no API key, under 3 requests per second) against E-utilities and GEO's own record pages. The question: does GEO's last-update date, as Entrez searches it, move for every change the store cares about? What does it miss, and what cheap second signal catches the miss?

Evidence classes, as in the earlier notes. Measured means a query or script ran on 2026-11-06 and the number is its output. Read means an official page or API says it. Inferred means reasoned from measured or read facts and not checked directly.

The pilots are in docs/research/geo-change-detection/ (es.py is the shared helper; exp.py to exp4.py are the four pilots below). They read the brief SOFT text of one record from https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=<ACC>&targ=self&form=text&view=brief.

Verdict¶

Use the Entrez Update Date field, open-ended from the last refresh, with a few days of overlap:

esearch db=gds term: gse[ETYP] AND <last refresh - 3 days>:3000[UDAT]

Intersect the result with the target list (the sequencing Series). Then re-fetch two more sets the date does not cover:

  1. Every record named by a "Reanalysis of", "SuperSeries of" or "SubSeries of" line in a Series fetched this refresh. A reanalysis released after its link was made adds a "Reanalyzed by" line to the original sample, and the original's date does not move at release (measured, one case, below). The refresh fetches these new Series anyway, so the targets cost nothing to find.
  2. The Series missing from the renewed geo-series snapshot are withdrawn. A date search can't see a deletion (inferred: a deleted record has no date to search).

Diffing the old and new geo-series snapshot rows (sample count, sample list, relations, supplementary file types, PubMed IDs) is a free backup check for the same Series, because a refresh renews that snapshot anyway (inferred; not tested against a real miss).

Cons: the sample is small (about 300 records read). The relation miss rests on one reanalysis Series. GEO repacked some _RAW.tar archives in place without moving the date (3 of 120 Series, measured); the store's filelist.txt row for the archive then goes stale in time and size, though no member file changed. Catching that would need a slow rolling re-fetch of file lists, and it is probably not worth it.

1. What Entrez offers¶

  • The gds database has two searchable date fields: PDAT "Publication Date" and UDAT "Update Date" (read: einfo db=gds, version 2.0, field list). There is no modification date in the datetype=mdat sense.
  • The esummary record of a Series has no update date (measured, esummary db=gds for GSE135893). Its fields are pdat, n_samples, samples, relations, extrelations (the SRA link), suppfile (file types only), pubmedids, bioproject and titles. So the geo-series snapshot the store already renews can't give the date; only a search can.
  • The index is current to the day. einfo gave a last-update stamp of 2026/11/06 09:47 on the day of the queries (measured).
  • UDAT holds only the newest update. In October 2026, 2,378 Series were released (PDAT), but only 2,369 of those also have an October UDAT (measured): the other 9 were updated again in November. So a fixed calendar-month window misses re-updated Series; the window must run from the last refresh to now (inferred from the counts).
  • NCBI's E-utilities and GEO help pages answered with a robot check page when fetched from this machine, so the datetype, mindate and maxdate docs were not re-read for this note. The range syntax <from>:<to>[UDAT] is the documented Entrez search form and it worked (measured: Entrez echoed it as 2026/10/01[UDAT] : 2026/10/31[UDAT]).

All measured on 2026-11-06. A monthly refresh re-fetches roughly 2,000 to 3,000 old Series across all of GEO, before cutting to sequencing Series (inferred from the table). The gdstype phrase "high throughput sequencing" matches nothing as a GTYP phrase search (0 hits, measured), so filter on the target list, not in the query.

2. Does the search date match the record's own date?¶

exp.py, part A: of the 417 Series with UDAT 2026/10/15, a random 40 were read. All 40 have !Series_last_update_date = Oct 15 2026 (measured).

exp4.py: 37 Series released on 2026/10/15 were absent from that day's UDAT list. Their SOFT dates fell on five later days (Oct 16, 19, 26, 30 and Nov 4). All 37 are in the UDAT list for their own SOFT date (measured). So the search date and the SOFT date agree on 77 of 77 Series across six days.

3. Which changes move the Series date¶

Change Moves the Series date? Evidence
Sample edited Yes. The Series date is the same day or later measured
Release from private Yes. The date is on or after the release day measured
New sample or other edit by the submitter Yes, as above inferred
Related record made (reanalysis, SuperSeries) Yes, on the day the link is made measured, 1 case
Linked reanalysis released later No measured, 1 case
GEO repacks _RAW.tar No measured, 3 of 120
Contact details edited Unknown; not biological metadata read

Edited sample (exp.py, part B). 40 random samples with UDAT 2026/10/15 link to 50 Series. 44 of those Series also carry Oct 15; the other 6 carry Oct 16 (measured). None carries an earlier date. A sample edit therefore moves its Series, sometimes a day later. The open-ended window covers that.

Release from private (exp2.py, part R). All 124 Series released on 2026/10/15 have a last-update date on or after that day: 87 the same day, 37 later (measured). GEO's update page says a Series' release date change propagates to its private samples (read: "Updating your GEO records", page dated July 16, 2024, via a public mirror).

Supplementary files (exp2.py, part S). 120 random Series were read (drawn from three random pages of the Series list, so most were last updated in 2019 to 2022). For each, the dates in the suppl/ folder listing were compared with the Series date. 115 had no file newer than the Series date. 3 had GSExxx_RAW.tar and filelist.txt dated 2024-02-01, after the Series date (GSE159955, GSE159540, GSE157241; measured). In GSE159955's filelist.txt the member files keep their 2020 dates; only the archive row is new. This looks like a GEO-side repack on one day, not a submitter change (inferred). The soft/ folder date is also regenerated by GEO (GSE135893: folder 2026-08-21, Series date 2025-02-21; measured), so download-site folder dates are no change signal.

Relation lines. In part S, 23 "SubSeries of" and 11 "SuperSeries of" partners were all released on or before the Series' own date: no miss (measured). exp3.py read every Series released on 2026/10/13 to 2026/10/15 for "Reanalysis of" lines and found one: GSE343291, submitted Aug 11, released Oct 14, reanalysing 14 samples from GSE138001 and GSE79238. Each of the 14 samples now carries "Reanalyzed by: GSE343291". Their dates, and both parent Series' dates, are Aug 12 2026, not Oct 14 (measured). The date moved when the link was made, while the reanalysis was private. If GEO hides the back-link until the reanalysis is public, a refresh between Aug 12 and Oct 14 saved the samples without the line, and no later date change prompts a re-fetch (inferred: whether GEO shows the line before release was not checked).

SRA links. Not tested. A sample's SRA relation line is usually in place before release (inferred). The snapshot diff on extrelations would show a late one.

4. The second signal¶

Signal Catches Cost
Relation targets of Series fetched this refresh late "Reanalyzed by", "SubSeries of" lines none: from SOFT already fetched
geo-series snapshot, uid set diff withdrawn Series none: snapshot renewed anyway
geo-series snapshot, row diff sample list, SRA link, file types, PubMed ID changes one join of two snapshots
Rolling re-fetch of a slice of filelist.txt GEO repacks of _RAW.tar about 1/12 of file lists a month

The first two are recommended (inferred from sections 3 and 1). The row diff is a cheap backstop. The rolling re-fetch is optional: the only miss it fixes is an archive's time and size, with member files unchanged.

Sources¶

  • E-utilities einfo, esearch and esummary on db=gds, queried 2026-11-06 (first-party API).
  • GEO record pages (acc.cgi, brief SOFT) and the GEO download site folder listings, read 2026-11-06.
  • GEO, "Updating your GEO records" (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/info/update.html, page dated July 16, 2024), read through a public mirror because the live page answered with a robot check.
