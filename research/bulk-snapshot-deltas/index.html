Can the bulk snapshots be renewed without a full download?¶

Checked 2026-11-06 against NCBI's download site (https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), E-utilities and the public SRA metadata bucket on AWS. The question: for each bulk snapshot the store takes (scripts/bulk/download_ncbi_bulk.py), does NCBI publish a daily, incremental or dated-delta version, how big is the full file, and should a renewal fetch the full file or a delta?

Measured means a listing, a HEAD request, a ranged read of the first 20 MB, or an E-utilities query was run on 2026-11-06 and the number is its output. Read means an official page or file says it. Inferred means reasoned from measured or read facts and not checked directly. No large file was downloaded.

Verdict¶

Snapshot Full size Delta from NCBI? Recommendation
SRA_Accessions.tab 35.6 GB No delta in this format. A daily livelist.csv.gz exists, but it lacks the Study and Experiment columns the store reads Full re-download
bioproject.xml 4.2 GB No file delta. E-utilities has no modification date for BioProject, only registration date Full re-download
biosample_set.xml.gz 5.0 GB No file delta. E-utilities can list records by modification date ([MDAT]) Full re-download
geo-series (E-utilities dump) about 300,000 records, built by the script No file at all. E-utilities can list Series by update date ([UDAT]) Delta by [UDAT], with a periodic full rebuild
SRA metadata Parquet (S3) 14.2 GB in 30 objects No. One current snapshot, replaced daily, no history Full re-download

Only geo-series gains from a delta: a week of changes is about 1,000 Series, a handful of E-utilities requests instead of about 630 (inferred). For the four files, a full fetch is minutes of bandwidth on NCBI's download site, while any delta would either miss columns or move load onto E-utilities, which the store wants to keep light (inferred).

2. SRA: what exists beside SRA_Accessions.tab¶

Daily and monthly metadata tarballs (sra/reports/Metadata/)¶

  • NCBI_SRA_Metadata_Full_YYYYMMDD.tar.gz appears monthly, 13 kept, from 2025-10-17 to 2026-10-13, 14–17 GB each (measured, listing).
  • NCBI_SRA_Metadata_YYYYMMDD.tar.gz appears daily, with some gaps, back to 2025-09-30, about 7.0 GB each in the last month (measured, listing; 7,529,135,959 bytes for 2026-11-06).
  • Contents (measured, first 20 MB of each, read as a tar stream): one folder per SRA submission (DRA…, ERA…, SRA…) holding submission, study, sample, experiment and run XML, plus a whole copy of SRA_Run_Members (4,267,387,363 bytes, the same size as the standalone file).
  • The daily tarball is not a one-day delta. It stays about 7.0 GB every day from 2026-10-14 to 2026-11-06 (measured), and its first 20 MB move forward in submission number between those two days (measured: median SRA2559506 against SRA2580028). It holds recently changed submissions plus the full run-members table (inferred). Its exact window is not documented on any NCBI page found (read, absence).
  • Either way it is submission XML, not the SRA_Accessions.tab table, so it cannot patch that file (inferred).

Daily mirroring deltas (sra/reports/Mirroring/)¶

  • One folder per day, NCBI_SRA_Mirroring_YYYYMMDD/, 46 of them back to 2026-09-22, and a monthly _Full folder (2026-10-01 and 2026-11-01) (measured, listing). The window rolls: older days are removed (inferred from the 46-day span).
  • A daily folder holds livelist.csv.gz (1.2 MB on 2026-11-05) and meta_*_set.xml.gz deltas for study, sample, experiment, run and analysis (167 KB–2.3 MB each) (measured). Each _Full livelist.csv.gz is 1.6 GB (measured).
  • The 2026-11-05 livelist.csv.gz has 125,893 rows: 45,467 runs, 38,893 experiments, 37,879 samples, 2,724 analyses and 930 studies (measured). Its rows carry LastUpdate dates of the day before, so it is a true one-day delta (measured on the first rows; inferred for all).
  • Its columns: Accession, Type, Status, Received, Published, LastUpdate, LastMetaUpdate, ReplacedBy, BioSample, BioProject, Insdc (measured).
  • SRA_Accessions.tab has Accession, Submission, Status, Updated, Published, Received, Type, Center, Visibility, Alias, Experiment, Sample, Study, Loaded, Spots, Bases, Md5sum, BioSample, BioProject, ReplacedBy (measured, first line).
  • The store reads Type, BioProject, Study, Experiment, Accession and Status (read, labdata.store.fetch.experiments_under and labdata.store.build). The livelist has no Study or Experiment, so it cannot rebuild the run-to-experiment and study links (inferred).

3. BioProject and BioSample through E-utilities¶

Query Count Evidence
db=biosample, all records 60,783,656 measured
db=biosample, 2026/10/30:2026/11/05[MDAT] 183,265 measured
db=bioproject, all records 1,115,121 measured
db=bioproject, 2026/10/30:2026/11/05[DATE] (registration) 2,100 measured
db=bioproject, [MDAT] or datetype=mdat not a field: E-utilities reads it as free text and finds nothing measured
  • einfo lists the date fields: BioSample has PDAT and MDAT (modification date); BioProject has only DATE (registration date) (measured).
  • So a BioProject delta from E-utilities catches new projects, not edits to old ones. A BioSample delta is possible: about 0.3% of records change in a week (measured counts), about 370 efetch batches of 500 per week (inferred). That moves the load from one 5 GB file to hundreds of E-utilities calls, which runs against the store's choice to stay gentle on NCBI (inferred).

4. geo-series¶

  • No GEO Series dump exists on the download site; the script builds it from esearch and esummary on db=gds (read, the script).
  • gse[ETYP] matches 300,226 Series (measured). A full dump is about 31 esearch pages of 10,000 and about 600 esummary batches of 500 (inferred from the script's batch sizes).
  • einfo for gds lists PDAT and UDAT (update date) (measured). gse[ETYP] AND 2026/10/30:2026/11/05[UDAT] matches 996 Series; the same week by [PDAT] matches 688 (measured).
  • A delta: query [UDAT] from the last snapshot's date, esummary those IDs, and replace their lines (inferred). It does not see Series that were withdrawn or made private, so a periodic full rebuild is still needed (inferred). Whether every field change in esummary moves UDAT is not checked.

5. Full download times¶

  • SRA_Accessions.tab: about 7 minutes (measured earlier on a lab node, outside this note). That is about 85 MB/s.
  • At the same rate, bioproject.xml and biosample_set.xml.gz take about 1 minute each, and the Parquet snapshot about 3 minutes (inferred, not timed).
  • geo-series: at least 1 minute of request time at the 10 per second E-utilities limit with a key; real wall time is set by server latency and was not timed here (inferred).

Cons and open points¶

  • No NCBI page found documents the daily metadata tarball's window, the mirroring folders, or how long either is kept. The facts above come from listings and partial reads.
  • The livelist's one-day nature is checked on its first rows only.
  • Download times other than SRA_Accessions.tab are extrapolated from one rate.

What was run¶

  • Directory listings and HEAD requests over HTTPS on the paths above.
  • Ranged reads of the first 20 MB of three tarballs (2026-10-13 Full, 2026-10-14 and 2026-11-06 daily), decompressed as a stream to list tar members.
  • One whole download of the 1.2 MB 2026-11-05 livelist.csv.gz, and the first line of SRA_Accessions.tab and the _Full livelist by ranged read.
  • esearch and einfo on biosample, bioproject and gds, and an S3 list-type=2 listing of the Parquet bucket.
